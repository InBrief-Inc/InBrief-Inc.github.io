Before you ask

The questions everyone sends first.

Do I need a card to start?
No. Free is $0 and gives you a public status page with incidents, maintenance and the public badges and feeds. You add paid capabilities when you want them, and the builder shows the total before you commit to anything.
Can I put it on my own domain?
Yes. Point a DNS record at InBrief and the certificate is issued and renewed for you. The domain stays registered to you. It is a capability you switch on, not a reason to move up a plan.
Which languages can the page publish in?
English, Arabic, French and Spanish. You write an incident once in the language your team works in and it publishes in every language you have switched on, after you review the prepared translation. Arabic renders right-to-left with Arabic-Indic digits.
What happens if my whole platform is down?
That is what outage continuity is for. The customer-facing page is hosted away from the infrastructure it reports on, so when your own systems cannot answer, visitors still get a page carrying your message rather than a connection error.
Can I drive it from my own systems?
Everything the dashboard does, REST does. Open, update and resolve incidents, report events and database health, and verify signed webhooks. The contract is published as OpenAPI, the ledger is readable as JSON, RSS and Atom without a key, and an assistant can connect over MCP by URL. Start at the developer quickstart.
How do customers hear about an incident?
Email, Discord, Microsoft Teams, Telegram and browser push. One update reaches all of them. Your own responders can take monitor and certificate alerts in the Slack channels they already watch.
Can I run it inside my own perimeter?
Cloud is available today. On-premises and hybrid are published as deployment options and open when their support boundaries are ready. Hybrid keeps incidents in InBrief Cloud while operational event history stays where you choose.
What if I want to leave?
The account, the domain and the history stay yours. The incident ledger is public JSON, RSS and Atom, so you can read your own history out at any time without asking anyone, and the audit log is exportable.
