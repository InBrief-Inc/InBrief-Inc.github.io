Why teams move

Four things most status pages get wrong

Each of these is a capability you can switch on by itself — not a reason to buy a bigger plan.

💸

Pay for what you switch on

Tiered pricing makes you buy a bundle to reach one thing inside it — the custom domain sits in the tier above yours, so you pay for four capabilities you will never open to get the one you came for. Here every capability carries its own price, the builder shows the running total as you add and remove, and that total is what checkout charges.

Free $0 Start $10 Pro $20 or build your own →
🌍

Write the incident once, publish it everywhere

Choose the languages your page publishes in and InBrief prepares the translations automatically — you review them before anything goes out. Visitors pick their own with a toggle, and the choice sticks for the rest of the incident. One page, not an incident written twice.

🛟

A page that survives the outage it reports

A status page served from the infrastructure it reports on is the one page guaranteed to be down exactly when it matters. Outage continuity puts the customer-facing page somewhere else entirely, so visitors still get a page — carrying your message instead of a connection error.

🔌

Machine-drivable from the first day

Open, update and resolve incidents over REST. Verify signed webhooks. Read the public ledger as JSON, RSS or Atom. The full contract ships as OpenAPI, and the site publishes an llms.txt so coding agents can read it directly.

Four languages, properly

Arabic is a first-class language here

Most status pages are English-only, so a team serving a region that does not read English writes every incident twice — or writes it once and accepts that half the audience will not follow it.

Arabic is not a translation target bolted on. The page renders right-to-left, with Arabic-Indic digits, because a right-to-left page laid out left-to-right reads as broken to the people it is for.

The platform

Everything a status page actually needs

📊 Status pages

Your brand, logo, accent and history window. Run more than one page from one account.

🌐 Custom domains

Point a DNS record at InBrief; the certificate is issued and renewed for you.

📡 Monitoring

Website, port, DNS and cron-job checks, grouped into the services customers recognise.

🗄 Database health

Private connection and pool diagnostics — visible to you, never on the public page.

🚨 Incidents

Publish, update, resolve. Schedule maintenance ahead of time; write the postmortem after.

📬 Subscribers

Email, Discord, Microsoft Teams, Telegram and browser push — one update reaches all of them.

💬 Team alerts

Monitor and certificate alerts into the Slack channel your responders actually watch.

📈 Analytics

Page loads, visitor-days and subscription conversion, for each public page.

👥 Roles & audit

Owner, admin, member and viewer permissions, with an exportable log of who changed what.

Badges, widgets & feeds

Public embeds that never break

Unauthenticated, cached for five minutes, and a badge URL never 404s — an unknown slug or host renders the grey unknown badge, because a broken image in somebody else's README reads as your service being broken.
